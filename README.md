# Cantus & Chronicle

Cantus & Chronicle is a small explorer of the Church's sung repertoire. It opens to a
Gregorian chant in square-note notation, with synthesized playback, its Latin text,
an English translation, and the days or feasts on which it is sung. There is no quiz
and no scoring: read it, hear it, and follow where it belongs.

## Use it

- **▶ Hear it** plays the chant in a monophonic, free-rhythm synthesized voice and
  highlights the notation as it sounds.
- **Tap any note or word** to start playback from that point. **Tempo**, **Pitch**, and
  **Volume** sliders adjust playback and are remembered in your browser.
- **℣ Psalm verse** adds the Introit's psalm verse and *Gloria Patri*, in notation and
  text, where the chant has one.
- **About this chant** gives a short note on the chant's text, liturgical place, or
  history.
- **Used in the liturgy on** shows where the chant occurs in the modern or 1962
  calendar.
- **Other chants on this feast** follows the other parts of the same Mass.
- **🎲 Another chant** chooses another entry; browser Back and shareable
  `?chant=` links return to a chant you have already visited.

Everything runs locally in your browser. There are no accounts, tracking, recordings,
or downloads.

## Sources and rights

The melodies are public domain. Notation is drawn from the
[GregoBase](https://gregobase.selapa.net/) corpus, principally transcriptions of the
*Graduale Romanum*. English translations are original renderings in a traditional
register, rather than copied from copyrighted missal translations. Each chant names
its notation source on screen.

See [Sources & provenance](sources.html) for the full reader-facing account of the
notation, liturgical placement, translations, playback, and rights.

The notation is engraved in the browser by [Exsurge](https://github.com/frmatthew/exsurge),
an MIT-licensed open-source library; its license notice is included in
[`vendor/exsurge.LICENSE`](vendor/exsurge.LICENSE). This project is released under the
[MIT License](LICENSE).

## Found a mistake?

Corrections are welcome: a wrong note, Latin typo, translation that misses, or an
incorrect liturgical assignment. Please [open an issue](https://github.com/financeprofessormckee/Cantus-and-Chronicle/issues)
with the chant or feast, what looks wrong, and the source you are comparing against.
