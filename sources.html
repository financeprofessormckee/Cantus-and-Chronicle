Everything here is meant to be traceable. This page explains where the square-note notation comes from, how chants are connected with their days and feasts, who wrote the English translations, and how the page works.

The chant notation

The notation is drawn from the GregoBase chant database, a long-running community transcription of the printed chant books. GregoBase releases its chant transcriptions under CC0, a public-domain dedication.

Where a choice exists, the notation follows a transcription of the Graduale Romanum, most often the 1961 Solesmes edition. Where that engraving is unavailable, an Editio Vaticana transcription may be used instead; the source line on the chant says so.

Where a chant is sung

A chant can belong to more than one day or feast, and the modern calendar and the 1962 calendar do not always use the same repertoire. The connections shown here are based on the printed chant books and are checked against three useful references:

  • Divinum Officium for saints’ days and the Common of Saints;
  • gregorien.info for the modern temporal cycle; and
  • the Diocese of La Crosse sacred music resources as an Introit cross-check.

Repeated chants are intentional when the Graduale itself repeats the same text. The explorer shows those connections rather than treating them as duplicates or omissions.

The English translations

The English beneath each chant is an original rendering made for this project, in a traditional register informed by the Douay-Rheims. It is not copied from the Roman Missal (ICEL) or the Gregorian Missal (Solesmes), whose translations are copyrighted.

Draft translations were prepared with language-model assistance from the Latin actually sung and checked against public-domain Douay-Rheims and Clementine Vulgate texts. Drafts that did not answer to the Latin were discarded.

About this chant

Some chants include a short optional note about their text, place in the liturgy, or history. These notes are based on the chant’s Latin and Scripture, the Mass formulary, and carefully identified background sources. They are written with language-model assistance from checked evidence, not from a model asked to supply facts or citations on its own.

How the page works

This is a plain web page. There is no account, tracking, or software to install; the notation, audio, and navigation all happen in your browser.

  • Exsurge, an MIT-licensed open-source engraver, draws the square notation.
  • Playback is synthesized, not a recording. It follows the score and highlights the syllable that is sounding.
  • Calendar relationships are bundled with the page, so exploring a chant does not require a third-party service.

Rights

The chant melodies are public domain. GregoBase notation is released under CC0 and is cited chant by chant. Published choir recordings and copyrighted missal translations are not included. The site’s synthesized playback is not a recording of any choir or edition.

Found a mistake?

Corrections are genuinely welcome: a wrong note, Latin typo, translation that misses, or an incorrect liturgical assignment. Open an issue on GitHub and say which chant or feast you were viewing, what looks wrong, and what you expected instead. Naming the source you are comparing against is especially helpful.
